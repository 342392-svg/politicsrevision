Learn faster: a practical revision system

Best default: 25 minutes of focused study, 5 minutes off. For each case, retrieve the fact, explain its significance, and give one limitation. Revisit after 1, 3, 7 and 14 days. These are useful revision intervals, not a guaranteed optimal schedule.

Four-pass evidence method

Pass 1 — Encode: read 6–8 cases and reduce each to a date, fact and argument.

Pass 2 — Retrieve: use flashcards without looking at answers.

Pass 3 — Evaluate: give a counterargument for each case.

Pass 4 — Apply: write a short PEEL paragraph linking two cases to a question.

14-day rotation

Days 1–4: UK government and politics.

Days 5–8: US government, rights and comparison.

Days 9–11: core ideologies and your chosen optional ideology.

Days 12–13: interleaved recall across all three papers.

Day 14: timed 9- and 25-mark practice; audit weak evidence.

If you have longer, repeat the cycle with deeper reading and updated examples.

How to turn a case into a top-level paragraph

Claim: The judiciary can meaningfully constrain executive action. Evidence: In Miller II (2019), the Supreme Court held prorogation unlawful. Explanation: Judicial review protected Parliament's constitutional functions. Challenge: Courts cannot ordinarily invalidate an Act of Parliament, and critics questioned judicial involvement. Judgement: The case proves a real, but bounded, judicial check on the executive.

For 25-mark extracts, analyse the extracts themselves; outside examples should support evaluation, not replace direct engagement with the supplied arguments.

UK–US comparative politics

AQA requires structural, rational and cultural approaches. Always identify a similarity, a difference, a reason and an evaluative judgement.

Three analytical lenses: Structural = institutions and constitutional rules; rational = strategic choices and incentives of political actors; cultural = political beliefs, traditions and historical expectations. Use more than one when the question allows.

Specification and further reading

Paper 1: UK government (constitution, Parliament, PM and cabinet, judiciary, devolution) and UK politics (democracy, elections, parties, pressure groups, EU).

Paper 2: US government and politics, plus UK–US comparison using structural, rational and cultural approaches.

Paper 3: Liberalism, conservatism and socialism plus one optional ideology chosen from nationalism, feminism, multiculturalism, anarchism or ecologism. This library includes all five for breadth, but check which one your teacher has selected.

Assessment: each paper is 2 hours, 77 marks and one third of the A-level.
